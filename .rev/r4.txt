**Things I checked that are correct** — worth stating, since a clean verdict is also a result:

- **Async poll loop CPU / scale.** A never-final child costs 0.016 s CPU per wall second; 100 concurrent actors cost 0.188 s CPU per 2 s wall (~9%). Not a busy-wait problem. `stop()` on a parent with a never-final child returns in 1 ms, leaves 0 stray tasks, and the poll task does not block shutdown. The `finally` teardown correctly stops the child and pops it from `_actors` (verified: `child status: stopped, actors: 0`).
- **Sync double-fire.** No double onDone. The blocking path calls `_queue_actor_done` and returns before the `_runner` finally exists; the non-blocking path fires only from the finally. Measured `hits: ['done.invoke.kid']` — exactly once.
- **`_queue_actor_done` re-entrancy.** Calling `self.send()` from the actor thread while the parent is mid-`send` does not deadlock: the `_is_processing` guard makes the background call return early and the owning thread drains the queued event. I probed the lost-wakeup window (event queued after the drain loop but before the flag clears) and could only trigger it by monkeypatching a 300 ms artificial stall into `_process_event_queue`; across ~800 unpatched trials with parallel regions, slow entry actions, slow `always` actions, and `setswitchinterval(1e-9)`, it never stranded an event. I'm not reporting it as a finding — it is theoretically present but I could not prove it reachable.
- **Deep-history correctness.** Configuration is right in both engines for: deep visited, deep unvisited, shallow visited, shallow unvisited, full parallel restore, and partial parallel restore (region left at its initial). No double-leaf-per-region. Entry order within a branch is correctly ancestor-before-descendant.
- **`stop_child` on an invoked machine** correctly does *not* fire onDone in either engine.
- **Full suite:** 2678 passed.

The two HIGH findings share a theme: the sync engine's `reached_final` predicate is the correct completion test, and the async engine doesn't use it — while the sync engine's use of it introduced an unsynchronised cross-thread read. Fixing both means one shared, snapshot-based helper.
